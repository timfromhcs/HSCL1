## 1. What "NP-hard" and "hardest" can honestly mean here
- **NP-complete** problems are the decision problems that are in NP and NP-hard; Karp's 1972 list has 21 of them (SAT is the first, via Cook 1971). Zuckerman (1996) showed that constrained optimisation versions of all 21 cannot be approximated within a constant factor unless P = NP (Wikipedia summary). Source: [Karp's 21 NP-complete problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karp%27s_21_NP-complete_problems).
- **"Hardest" is a property of instances and of solvers, not of a problem name.** For random 3-SAT the hard region is near the clause/variable ratio of about 4.26 (cavity-method estimate 4.26675 for large n; rigorous bounds only 3.26 to 4.506); the peak is solver dependent and finite-size effects shift it. Sources: [cond-mat/0207194](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0207194), [Mu and Hoos slides](https://cs.ubc.ca/~w-esa/ESA/Publ/MuHoo15_IJCAI_slides.pdf), [cs/0504101](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0504101v1), [The Plot Thickens](https://www.dcs.gla.ac.uk/~pat/jchoco/trianglePacking/papers/the%20PlotThickens.pdf). A claim "hardest ever" is never made by this repository; a benchmark reports the instance family, parameters, seed and what was measured.
- **Beyond NP** (kept as `PLANNED`, not built): model counting #SAT is #P-complete; counting QBF strategies is #PSPACE-complete; DQBF model counting is #EXP-complete (satisfiability NEXP-complete). Sources: [arXiv 2511.07337](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07337v2), [ECCC 2026/101](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/101/download), [AAAI #SAT](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16486/16293), model counting competition [2012.01323](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.01323v1.pdf).

## 2. Families with established instance sources
| Family | Established source / generator | What gives hardness | Ground truth available |
|---|---|---|---|
| SAT, random k-SAT | uniform generator: k distinct variables per clause, random sign each (cs/0504101); ratio about 4.26 for k=3 | phase transition | none in general; verify models, check UNSAT proofs |
| SAT, competition | [SAT Competition 2026](https://satcompetition.github.io/2026/benchmarks.html): DIMACS `p cnf` with exact counts, PAR-2 ranking, 20 new instances per team, instance description required; the 2018 edition required DRAT proofs; the 2026 page says several proof checkers can be chosen (format of 2026 **not confirmed**); see also [Froleyks, Yu, Biere 2025 benchmarks](https://cca.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/FroleyksYuBiere-SAT-Competition-2025-benchmarks.pdf) | structure of real instances | proofs |
| SAT, planted / cryptographic | integer-factoring circuits as planted-solution benchmarks ([arXiv 2604.09837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.09837)); reduced-round SHA-1/SHA-256 preimage ([Zaikin 2026](https://olegzaikin.github.io/publications/2026-z-constraints-preprint.pdf): 19 rounds of SHA-256 and 24 of SHA-1 as reference points; Transalg and Nejati encoders) | arithmetic structure | planted factors; prime N gives a known UNSAT |
| MaxSAT | [MaxSAT Evaluation](https://maxsat-evaluations.github.io) (MSE 2026 is the 20th edition; anytime and complete tracks; weighted and unweighted) | optimisation gap | best-known costs |
| Graph colouring, clique | Second DIMACS Challenge (1992-93): 137 colouring instances, 80 clique instances; clique optima proven for 77; colouring best-known tables, "difficult 36/18" subset; running tables at [Mascia/ULB](https://iridia.ulb.ac.be/~fmascia/maximum_clique/) | structure (queens, Mycielski, latin squares) | best-known values |
| Hamiltonian cycle | [FHCP Challenge Set](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1902.10352): 1001 structurally difficult Hamiltonian graphs (66 to 9528 vertices), solved 985 by the winners; HCP to TSP conversion ([1806.09285](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.09285)); fhcp.edu.au | few Hamiltonian cycles, structure | every instance is Hamiltonian, a cycle is given |
| TSP | TSPLIB (optimal tours as `.opt.tour`); Concorde closed all but four of the 110 instances of its benchmark page, [Concorde benchmarks](https://math.uwaterloo.ca/tsp/concorde/benchmarks/bench99.html); [DIMACS TSP Challenge](https://archive.dimacs.rutgers.edu/Challenges/TSP/download.html) with Held-Karp bounds | size, structure | optimal values, lower bounds |
| Knapsack, subset sum | David Pisinger's generator for hard instances ([codes page](https://hjemmesider.diku.dk/~pisinger/codes.html), "Where are the hard knapsack problems?", C&OR 2005): strongly correlated, inverse strongly correlated, almost strongly correlated, multiple strongly correlated, spanner | LP/IP gap | DP gives the optimum for small ranges |
| MIP | [MIPLIB 2017](https://miplib.zib.de/): 1065 instances, 240-instance benchmark set, easy/hard/open status; only 70 of 134 once-unsolved instances remain open ([Gleixner et al.](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s12532-020-00194-3)) | mixed | optimum or open |

## 3. Design rules for the benchmark code (from the research and from the CQ-HECS audit)
1. **A solver answer is never the evidence.** SAT: the model is checked against the CNF by independent code. UNSAT: a proof is checked by an independent checker, otherwise the verdict is `UNKNOWN` (the convention of `cqhecs/sat.py`, broken by `sat_external.py`/`unified.py`, see `HCSPNP_BASELINE.md` C1-C4). Optimisation: the witness is re-evaluated (tour length, colouring validity, clique validity, knapsack weight and profit) by code that shares nothing with the solver.
2. **Instances carry their provenance:** family, parameters, seed, generator version, SHA-256 of the written file, and `expected` (`SAT`, `UNSAT`, a known optimum or bound, or `null`). A verdict that contradicts `expected` is a recorded `disagreement`, not a pass.
3. **Strict, shared input rules** (audit P1): DIMACS must have the header, exact clause count, digits only, size caps. The benchmark parser is the reference for a later differential test against the Python and native parsers of CQ-HECS.
4. **Timeouts are `UNKNOWN`, never a verdict.** PAR-2 scoring is reported (as in the SAT Competition) next to the raw times.
5. **Honest scale:** pure Python solvers are baselines for correctness and for small to medium sizes; they are not competitive with Kissat or CaDiCaL and the report never says they are. External solvers are reached only through an adapter that reports `unavailable` instead of falling back silently.
6. **Light by default.** The default suite finishes in seconds on the host; heavy sweeps are opt-in and take the heavy-job lock of the scheduler.
